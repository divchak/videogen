**Deep Legend Analysis (for video authenticity):**  
Sundarar, born in sacred lineage, was arranged to marry a virtuous girl. As the procession neared the temple town of Thiruvennainallur, an elderly Brahmin (Shiva in disguise) blocked the path with his staff, declaring the groom was his hereditary slave (adimai) given generations ago. The families protested. The old man produced an ancient palm-leaf document as proof. The matter was taken before the village assembly and then to the Shiva temple. There, in front of the Lingam, the old man transformed into the resplendent Lord with matted locks, crescent moon, Ganga, third eye, Uma seated beside Him on Nandi. The assembly prostrated. Young Sundarar, overcome with recognition, shame at his worldly attachment, and overwhelming love, poured out this first hymn — acknowledging he had always belonged to Shiva and could never again claim independence. This "enslavement" became his greatest liberation and the beginning of his divine poetic outpouring. The song is therefore not just poetry but the living record of a soul's awakening at the exact moment God personally claimed him.
