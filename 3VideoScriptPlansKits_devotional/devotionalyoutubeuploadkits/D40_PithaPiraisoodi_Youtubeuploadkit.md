🔱 ABOUT THIS SACRED HYMN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
"Pitha Pirai Soodi" (பித்தா பிறைசூடி) translates as an intimate address to the Lord: "O Madman wearing the crescent moon! O Great Lord! Giver of Grace!" Far from an insult, "Pitha" (madman) is an ecstatic celebration of divine madness that completely shatters human calculation.

Composed by Sundaramurthi Nayanar (7th century CE) at approximately 16 years of age, this is his very first pathigam. As detailed in the Periyapuranam, an elderly Brahmin (Shiva in disguise) interrupted Sundarar's wedding procession, producing a palm-leaf document claiming the youth belonged to him as an ancestral slave. Upon uncovering the truth at the temple, the Lord manifested in full cosmic glory, inspiring this eternal poem of joyful surrender[cite: 1, 2].

This hymn describes Lord Shiva at Thiruvennainallur Arulthurai—one of the holiest Paadal Petra Sthalams[cite: 1, 2]. The lyrics paint the beautiful paradox of the soul's relationship to God:
🔱 The Lord who personally interrupts worldly attachments to claim his devotee[cite: 1, 2]
🌊 The one with the sacred Ganga flowing through his matted locks (Jata)[cite: 1, 2]
🌙 The one adorned with the silver crescent moon (Bala Chandra)[cite: 1, 2]
👁️ The one whose third eye radiates soft golden light[cite: 1, 2]
🐂 The one who rides the sacred white bull Nandi[cite: 1, 2]
💃 The Lord who places Himself permanently within the mind of the seeker[cite: 1, 2]
